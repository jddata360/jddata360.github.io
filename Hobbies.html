Rubik's Cube

Speed Cubing Pattern Recognition CFOP Method

Speed cubing is where logic meets muscle memory. I have been solving the 3x3 Rubik's Cube for several years and use the CFOP (Cross, F2L, OLL, PLL) method. The satisfaction of watching 43 quintillion possibilities collapse into order in under a minute is something truly unique — it is also a great way to sharpen algorithmic thinking, which translates directly into my work as a data analyst.

Mini Canvas Art

Acrylic Painting Mini Canvas Landscape & Abstract

Painting on mini canvases is my way of slowing down and being present. Working within a small frame — often just 4x4 inches — forces creative economy: every brushstroke has to count. I work primarily with acrylics, exploring landscapes, abstract patterns, and occasionally French-inspired scenes (a nod to my Francophone interests). Each piece takes 1–3 hours and is entirely hand-painted.

Instrumentalist

Kalimba Ukelele Fingerstyle

Music is a form of data too — patterns, rhythm, and structure. I play the Kalimba (thumb piano) and Ukelele, both self-taught. The Kalimba is particularly meditative; its bright, harp-like tones are well-suited to ambient and folk melodies. I enjoy learning pieces by ear and occasionally composing short original pieces. What These Say About Me

My hobbies are not unrelated to my professional life — they reflect the same underlying traits. Speed cubing builds algorithmic thinking and pattern recognition. Painting develops attention to detail and an eye for visual communication (useful in dashboards and data storytelling). Music builds patience and the habit of iterative improvement. Together, they make me a more creative, focused, and well-rounded analyst.
