About the Data

📊

Project Goal

Utilise most Power BI features learned through online courses to curate, analyse, and visualise an open-source Netflix dataset — going beyond day-to-day work to build something that fully showcases analytical capability.

This dashboard is built around four core questions:

What is the data about? How can it be categorised? Are there dependencies within categories? What is the outcome?

Results

This dashboard provides a comprehensive analysis of the Netflix dataset, covering key metrics and trends related to shows and movies. The design uses a dark theme with prominent visuals to make the data easy to interpret.

Visualizations — Middle Row

Three bar charts that break down the data by key categories.

Rating by Total Shows

A horizontal bar chart showing distribution by maturity rating. TV-MA leads, followed by TV-14 and TV-PG — indicating a Netflix library geared predominantly toward mature audiences.

Genres by Total Shows

Dramas & International Movies is the top genre, followed by Documentaries and Stand-Up Comedy — reflecting a strong platform presence for non-fiction and comedic content.

Genres by Duration

Total duration per rating category — offering an alternative lens on content volume distribution that goes beyond simple title count.

Visualizations — Bottom Row

Geographical distribution and time-series analysis providing deeper context.

🗺️

Total Shows by Country

A world map with darker red shading for higher content concentration. USA and India lead — reflecting Netflix's key markets and largest content libraries.

🍩

Movies vs. TV Shows

Donut chart breakdown: 6.13K Movies (69.62%) vs. 2.68K TV Shows (30.38%). Netflix's library is firmly film-dominant.

📈

Total Shows by Release Year

Line chart from 1940–2020 showing slow growth until the 2000s, then explosive acceleration — peaking in 2018 with 767 releases, reflecting Netflix's aggressive content strategy.
