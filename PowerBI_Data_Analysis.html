About the Data

My objective here is to utilise most Power BI features which I had learned using different online courses. I am curious about curating data and analysis. Such mini projects (self-made) allow me to think and progress. As part of my job I do regular data analysis and visualization, however I felt this isn't enough to showcase my talent. Hence I utilised an open-source platform to collect raw data to analyse and build a dashboard. This dashboard is built around a few key questions:

  • What is the data all about?
  • How can the data be categorized?
  • Within the categorization, is there any dependency or relation between the data?
  • What is the outcome?

Results

Netflix Data Analysis: This dashboard provides a comprehensive analysis of a Netflix dataset, covering key metrics and trends related to shows and movies. The design is clean, with a dark theme and prominent visuals that make the data easy to interpret.

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) — Top Row

The top row of the dashboard presents high-level summary statistics, providing an immediate overview of the entire dataset.

  • Total Shows: 8,800 — The total number of unique shows and movies in the dataset.
  • Total Ratings: 19 — The number of distinct maturity ratings (e.g., TV-MA, PG-13, TV-G) present in the data.
  • Genre Shows: 515 — The total number of unique genres available across all shows and movies.
  • Total Directors: 4,527 — The total number of unique directors represented in the dataset.
  • Start Year: 1925 — The earliest release year in the dataset, possibly a historical documentary or classic film added to the platform.
  • End Year: 2021 — The latest release year in the dataset, aligning with the typical end date for publicly available Netflix datasets.

Detailed Visualizations — Middle Row

This row features three bar charts that break down the data by key categories.

  • Rating by Total Shows: This horizontal bar chart shows the distribution of shows based on their maturity rating. TV-MA has the highest number of shows, followed by TV-14 and TV-PG, indicating that a significant portion of the Netflix library is geared toward mature audiences. Ratings such as PG-13, TV-Y7, TV-Y, and OK show progressively fewer shows.
  • Genres by Total Shows: This chart displays the top genres based on the number of shows. Dramas & International Movies is the leading genre, followed by Documentaries and Stand-Up Comedy, suggesting a strong presence of non-fiction and comedic content on the platform.
  • Genres by Duration: This bar chart shows the total duration (or number of titles) for each rating category, offering another view of content volume distribution across ratings.

Detailed Visualizations — Bottom Row

This row provides different perspectives on the data, including geographical distribution and a time-series analysis.

  • Total Shows by Country: This world map highlights countries with the most shows. Darker red shading indicates a higher concentration of content. North America (especially the USA) and parts of Europe and Asia (particularly India) have the highest number of shows, reflecting key markets where Netflix has a large content library.
  • Movies vs. TV Shows: This donut chart provides a simple breakdown of content type — 6.13K (69.62%) are Movies and 2.68K (30.38%) are TV Shows, showing that Netflix's library is predominantly film-based.
  • Total Shows by Release Year: This line chart visualises new releases over time, broken down by type (Movie vs. TV Show). The horizontal axis spans from 1940 to 2020. There is a slow, steady increase from the 1940s to the 1990s, followed by a massive surge in content from the early 2000s, with explosive growth peaking around 2018–2020. The peak year for releases was 2018 with 767 shows. This trend highlights Netflix's aggressive content acquisition and production strategy in recent years.
